About

A competition team, run by students

Millis Dynamics competes in the FIRST Tech Challenge: the same game, the same deadline, and the same field as every other team in the state.

What we do

Engineering with a scoreboard attached.

Robotics is the closest thing school offers to real engineering work: an open-ended problem, a hard deadline, a limited budget, and a competition at the end where the thing either works or it doesn’t.

Students on this team do the whole job. They read the game manual and argue about strategy. They model the robot in CAD, cut and assemble it, wire the control system, and write the code that drives it. They keep an engineering notebook that documents every decision, and they stand in front of judges to defend it.

Then they drive it: two-team alliances, thirty seconds of autonomous play, then two minutes of everything they practiced.

Team at a glance

  • Program: FIRST Tech Challenge (FTC)
  • Run by: FIRST
  • Based in: Millis, Massachusetts
  • Season: kickoff in September, states in late March
  • Open to: every Millis student, no experience required

The competition

How FTC works

One game per season, announced at kickoff in September and played by every FTC team in the world through to the championship.

The matches

Two-team alliances face off on a 12-foot field. Every match opens with thirty seconds of fully autonomous play before drivers take over, so the code matters as much as the driving.

The notebook

Teams document every design decision in an engineering notebook and defend it to judges. It is how the Inspire Award, the top award in FTC, gets won.

Scholarships

FIRST participation unlocks a large scholarship catalog, and a lot of it goes unclaimed every year simply because students never apply.

A year on the team

What the season looks like

  1. September

    Kickoff

    The season’s game is revealed on the second Saturday after Labor Day. Everybody starts from zero on the same day.

  2. Sep – Nov

    Design and build

    Strategy, prototyping, CAD, and code, plus the engineering notebook that runs alongside all of it.

  3. Dec – Mar

    Qualifying tournaments

    Massachusetts events through the winter, iterating on the robot between each one.

  4. Late March

    State championship

    The best teams in Massachusetts, and the judged awards.

  5. Spring

    FIRST Championship

    The world championship, for teams that advance out of states.

Commitment

How much time it takes

In season: a couple of afternoons a week, more in the run-up to a tournament, plus the occasional Saturday for the event itself.

Competition days are full days, and they are the best part of the year.

Exact meeting times get set around what students pick on the sign-up form, so the schedule fits real lives with sports, jobs, and homework in them.

Questions

Frequently asked

Do I need robotics experience to join?

No. Most people arrive having never built a robot, and the team teaches as it goes. If you are willing to learn and to show up, you are qualified. There is also plenty of room for people who would rather write, design, film, or handle sponsorship than turn a wrench.

What does it cost a student to join?

We are raising sponsorship and fundraising money specifically so that cost is not what keeps somebody off this team. We cannot promise a completely free season yet, and we will be straight with you about anything students are asked to cover.

If cost is a concern for you or your family, tell us early and we will work it out.

How much time does it take?

A couple of afternoons a week in season, ramping up before tournaments, plus competition Saturdays. Meeting times are set around what students choose on the sign-up form.

What do I actually learn?

CAD in Onshape, machining and assembly, wiring, programming in Blocks or Java, and the habit of documenting a design so somebody else can follow it. Also the part nobody advertises: how to fix something under time pressure while it matters.

Where are competitions?

Massachusetts qualifiers run from December through March and cluster east of Worcester (mostly the Boston area), generally under an hour from Millis. The state championship is in late March.

How can adults help?

Volunteer, or sponsor. Volunteers do not need to know FTC. Being an experienced adult in the room is the job, and FIRST publishes free mentor training. Sponsors keep the robot funded, and get their name on the shirt.
